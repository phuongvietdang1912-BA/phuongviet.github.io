Air Quality Forecasting with Deep Learning

This project develops and compares deep learning models for short-term PM2.5 air quality forecasting. The objective was to forecast the next 24 hours of PM2.5 values and evaluate whether recurrent neural networks can outperform a simple naïve baseline.

Business Problem

Air quality forecasting can support monitoring, planning, and early-warning decisions. However, predictive models must be evaluated carefully because a model that performs well on average may still fail during pollution spikes or unusual weather conditions.

Key Questions

  • Can deep learning improve 24-hour PM2.5 forecasting compared with a naïve baseline?
  • Which recurrent architecture performs best?
  • How does prediction error change across the 24-hour forecast horizon?
  • What are the practical limitations of the final model?

Data & Tools

  • Dataset: historical hourly air quality and weather-related observations
  • Tools: Python, Pandas, NumPy, TensorFlow, Keras, Matplotlib
  • Models: GRU, LSTM, stacked LSTM, baseline forecasting model
  • Metrics: MAE, RMSE, horizon-wise error

Approach

  1. Prepared hourly time-series data for supervised learning.
  2. Created lookback windows and direct 24-step forecast targets.
  3. Benchmarked multiple recurrent neural network architectures.
  4. Compared model results against a naïve baseline.
  5. Evaluated error across forecast horizons from 1 hour to 24 hours.
  6. Documented deployment limitations and model risk.

Key Results

  • The final GRU-128 model achieved approximately MAE 45.64 and RMSE 64.70.
  • The model improved error by approximately 35% compared with the naïve baseline.
  • Short-horizon predictions were more accurate than longer-horizon predictions.
  • Forecast error increased as the model predicted further into the future.

Business Interpretation

The model is useful for short-term trend monitoring and can support early operational awareness. However, it should not be treated as a fully automated public-health warning system without further validation, real-time monitoring, and testing under extreme pollution events.

Project Value

This project demonstrates my ability to design a forecasting experiment, compare model architectures, evaluate results properly, and explain model limitations in business language.
