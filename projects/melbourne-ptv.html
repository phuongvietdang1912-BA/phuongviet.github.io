Melbourne Public Transport Patronage Analysis

This project integrates four Victorian Government public transport datasets into a SQL warehouse to trace Melbourne's post-COVID patronage recovery across modes, stations, and day types. The datasets arrive at three different grains and schemas, so the work centres on reconciling them into a single, reliable analytical model.

Business Problem

COVID-19 caused an unprecedented collapse in Melbourne public transport patronage, and recovery has been uneven across modes, locations, and trip types. Decision-makers need to know which parts of the network have recovered, which remain below pre-COVID demand, and how travel patterns have structurally changed — to guide service planning and investment.

Key Questions

  • How far has patronage recovered toward pre-COVID (2018) levels, by mode?
  • Which modes recovered fastest, and which still lag?
  • Has the weekday commute recovered differently from weekend leisure travel?
  • Within the working week, are some days recovering more slowly than others?

Data & Tools

  • Datasets: four Victorian Government public-transport datasets (monthly patronage by mode, annual metro and regional station patronage, monthly average by day-type) — Department of Transport and Planning, CC BY 4.0
  • Scale: ~6,000 rows across four sources with three different grains
  • Tools: SQL Server, Python (data cleaning), dimensional modelling
  • Techniques: multi-source integration, schema reconciliation, grain alignment, conformed dimensions, window-function analysis, recovery indexing

Approach

  1. Collected four public datasets with different grains (monthly-by-mode, annual-by-station, monthly-by-day-type) and schemas (metro stations report 15 columns; regional stations only 6).
  2. Cleaned real-world mess in Python: BOM characters, trailing junk rows, thousand-separator numbers, inconsistent mode names across sources, and station-name normalisation.
  3. Built a four-layer SQL warehouse (raw, cleaned, dimensional, analytics) with conformed dimensions shared across three fact tables.
  4. Indexed patronage to a 2018 pre-COVID baseline so modes of different sizes could be compared on recovery.
  5. Used window functions for month-over-month, year-over-year, and day-of-week recovery analysis.

Key Insights

  • Recovery splits metro vs regional. Regional rail has not only recovered but exceeded pre-COVID patronage (~109% of 2018), while metropolitan train remains ~15% below baseline (~85%) and tram lags furthest (~78%).
  • The shortfall is the weekday commute, not travel in general. For metro train, weekend (leisure) travel recovered to ~89% of pre-COVID, but weekday (commute) travel plateaued at ~74% — a ~15-point gap.
  • Within the week, Monday and Friday lag mid-week. Metro train recovery is lowest on Monday and Friday (~70%) and highest Tuesday–Thursday (73–79%) — the structural signature of hybrid work, where office attendance clusters mid-week.
  • Findings are descriptive, not causal. The patterns are consistent with a shift to hybrid work, but other factors (regional population growth, service and fare changes) are not separated out.

Business Recommendations

  • Plan capacity around the new weekly shape: mid-week (Tue–Thu) demand recovered far more than Monday/Friday, so uniform weekday scheduling over-serves the week's edges.
  • Treat metro and regional separately in planning — they are on different recovery trajectories, and a single network-wide figure hides the divergence.
  • Separate weekday and weekend analysis in all reporting, since blended figures conceal the commute-vs-leisure gap that drives the story.
  • Use a pre-COVID baseline recovery index to communicate change clearly, rather than raw patronage counts that aren't comparable across modes.

Project Value

This project demonstrates my ability to integrate messy, multi-source public data into a reliable analytical model, reason carefully about mismatched data grains, and translate the results into a clear, current, and locally relevant finding — Melbourne's uneven, hybrid-work-shaped public-transport recovery.
